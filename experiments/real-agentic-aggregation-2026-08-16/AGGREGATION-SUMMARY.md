## Headline for Section 8

The Section 6 mechanism reproduces on the executed system, and the
perturbation family again owns both sign and size of the covariance
effect:

- **Tail-swap family (the article's study family):** declaring the three
  curves independent is roughly harmless — empirical width understatement
  ranges **−7.7 % to +6.7 %** across 4 arms x 4 composites; cross-covariance
  share of variance −9.6 % to +10.5 %.
- **Gaussian control family (the chain example's own noise family):**
  understatement is systematically positive — up to **+20.3 % empirical /
  +21.8 % delta** (stage B, random forest, RMS), cross-covariance share up
  to **+38.8 %**. The mean-curve correlations flip accordingly (e.g. stage A
  logit RGA–RGR −0.018 under tail-swap vs +0.383 under Gaussian).

So the executed system sits between the two Section 6 poles: its
volume-composite intervals are family-dependent in exactly the way the
mechanism section predicts — closer to harmless under tail-swap, material
(up to a fifth of the interval width) under the Gaussian family the
chain analysis itself uses.

## Caveats carried from the design

- Executed-system result, not a field study (the manuscript's standing
  §8 wording).
- The trained scorers are the disclosed stand-ins for the deterministic
  gates; composites inherit that disclosure.
- The Gaussian family's per-severity draw is fixed-draw (one seeded draw
  per severity level, resampled rows) — same scheme as the Section 6
  precompute; a redraw variant is a possible extension, noted, not run.
- The tail-swap curve is rebuilt on top of the pinned package's
  `rgr_score` exactly as in Section 6 (the package ships no tail-swap).
