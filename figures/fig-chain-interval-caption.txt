The composed compliance figure of a two-link agent chain, h = C_A * C_B with a
logistic-regression link followed by a random-forest link, measured on German
credit (OpenML credit-g, 700/300 split, n_test = 300) with B = 2000 paired
bootstrap replicates and the perturbations redrawn on every replicate, so the
cross-link correlation reported here (0.712) is unconditional rather than
conditional on one perturbation draw. The 95 per cent interval is computed twice
from the same replicates: (a) carrying the measured cross-link covariance, and
(b) with the cross-term declared zero, the naive independence assumption. Both
carry the plug-in point estimate 0.827; declaring the cross-term zero narrows the
interval from [0.787, 0.867] to [0.796, 0.857], understating its width by 23.6
per cent. The hatched band runs between the two lower endpoints: any threshold
in it is passed by the naive interval and left undecided by the honest one. It
is not the whole range over which (a) fails to decide, which is the whole of
(a), and it is not the whole set of thresholds the two readings judge
differently: a mirror band of the same width, not drawn, sits between the two
upper endpoints, where the naive interval fails and the honest one is again
undecided. Every number above is read from the run output; the one chosen number is
the decision threshold 0.791, which is illustrative -- a risk appetite is set by
the operator, not by the data -- and is placed inside that band, so the naive
interval sits clear of it and reads as passing while the interval that carries
the covariance straddles it and does not decide at this sample size.

Notes for the build. Numbers come from
papers/safe-composed-uncertainty/experiment/redraw-2026-07-24/results-redraw.json,
"chain" -> "redraw", produced against safeai pinned at 39768fcd5264c881f7174268
bbffda52b298ae89. Interval endpoints are stored in that file and are used as
given; nothing was derived from widths. The threshold is not in the file: the
script sets it to the midpoint of the two lower endpoints rounded to three
decimals (0.791) and asserts that it lies strictly between them, which is what
makes the two readings differ.
